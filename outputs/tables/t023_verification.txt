INTERPRETATION OF EACH DIAGNOSTIC
1. Definitions (descriptive): concentration specific to the regulated definition is consistent with targeting. Genuine deleveraging requires separate debt/economic-leverage evidence; alternative RL1 is only a denominator change, not economic leverage. Paired RL1 difference-in-bunching=0.06484553, CI [0.01736021,0.11528972], conditioned on the fixed unrounded cutoff. RL2 uses observed perpetual items only: pre/post N=132/99, firms=33/26, difference=-0.00505051 without inference because post has fewer than 30 firms.
2. Reporting dates (descriptive): unusual reporting-snapshot concentration, especially December, could support temporary reporting adjustment; durable real adjustment need not create a December peak. RL1 post below shares: June 34/254=13.39%, December 44/376=11.70%; excluding fiscal-year-flag firms, 34/250=13.60% and 44/370=11.89%. No special December peak. There are no off-report-date observations, and no formal June/December contrast test.
3. Reversal (selected, descriptive transitions): increased post-period near-bin returns above 0.70 relative to controls and pre would support temporary targeting. RL1 post 14/61=22.95%, control 9/50=18.00%; difference CI [-0.1071168,0.1977178]. Pre near 20/36=55.56%; post minus pre difference=-0.3260474, CI [-0.5365585,-0.1271132]. Thus reversal declined, without evidence of increased temporary reversal. Persistent compliance can reflect real or persistent cosmetic adjustment. RL2 post 6/17 versus 17/45 also has an imprecise contrast. Next-date gaps, mean reversion and right censoring prevent causal claims.
4. Composition (selected, descriptive transitions): debt declines support real adjustment; increasing cosmetic proxies without debt declines could indicate cosmetic channels. Entrant and stayer mean log-debt changes are -0.0325219 and -0.0348377 (difference=0.0023158, cluster p=0.9143832). Log-assets difference=0.0408122 (cluster p=0.004257), consistent with denominator expansion, not proof of cosmetic activity. Cash/assets, minority/equity and JV/assets do not have precise differences. Perpetual/equity uses items observed at both dates: 8 entries/42 stayers, 19 firms; no inference. Logs assume within-firm currency comparability.
5. Excess mass (descriptive): local mass at the regulated threshold relative to unaffected placebo thresholds can support threshold targeting. Genuine deleveraging can also target the regulatory threshold. Full RL1 main b: pre -0.8772 (SE 0.5981), post 3.1429 (SE 1.2667). Paired RL1: pre -0.7305 (SE 0.6669), post 3.6607 (SE 1.3665); paired alternative RL1 at rounded 0.53: pre -0.4030 (SE 0.5742), post 0.0039 (SE 0.6513). The paired ratios share N but use distinct definition-specific cutoffs; their difference is not formally tested.
After excluding the true regulated region, full-sample RL1 post main placebo b at 0.60 changes from -1.3254 to -0.3387 (SE 1.0048, CI [-1.9196,1.9123]); at 0.80 from -0.9079 to -0.0449 (SE 0.7308, CI [-1.3760,1.5029]). Both CIs include zero; some robustness placebo point estimates are positive. Their previous negative signs are not independent evidence of absent mass. They are sensitive to counterfactual fitting and provide only a limited descriptive comparison.
Assumptions: a smooth counterfactual outside excluded regions, suitable polynomial shape/windows, and comparable selection. No integration correction, elasticity or causal mechanism interpretation. Pointwise inference, no multiplicity correction and no formal pre/post b-difference test.
Principal diagnostics remain regulated-ratio placebo thresholds, reversal, reporting snapshots and composition. Taken together, the results are consistent with RL1 threshold targeting but do not identify cosmetic rather than real balance-sheet adjustment.
