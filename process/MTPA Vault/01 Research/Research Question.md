## Working research question (verbatim)

> Did China's Three Red Lines policy reduce property developers' economic leverage, or did it primarily shift liabilities off the consolidated balance sheet?

**Short version:** *real vs. cosmetic deleveraging.*

## Topic

China's "Three Red Lines" policy (三道红线) for property developers, introduced in August [number withheld] by the People's Bank of China (PBoC) and the Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development (MOHURD). Details: [[Three Red Lines Policy]].

## Motivation

- **Policy relevance:** hard thresholds on *reported* consolidated ratios create an incentive to manage the reported numbers rather than economic leverage (off-balance-sheet JVs, debt disguised as equity 明股实债, perpetuals booked as equity). Press reports describe such practices (see [[Literature Overview]]).
- **Gap:** existing studies examine direct effects on leverage, spreads, performance and spillovers; whether the thresholds were *circumvented* has, per the project's search so far, not been studied systematically ([[Literature Overview]]).
- **PI's choice of angle:** pensions deliberately excluded; a corporate-finance / balance-sheet focus is intended.

> [!idea] Suggestion (not decided) – testable hypotheses
> - **H1 (bunching):** after August [number withheld], excess mass just on the compliant side of each threshold, absent in [number withheld]–[number withheld].
