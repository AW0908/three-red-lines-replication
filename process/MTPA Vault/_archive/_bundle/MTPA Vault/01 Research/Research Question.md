## Working research question (verbatim)

> Did China's Three Red Lines policy reduce property developers' economic leverage, or did it primarily shift liabilities off the consolidated balance sheet?

**Short version:** *real vs. cosmetic deleveraging.*

## Topic

China's "Three Red Lines" policy (三道红线) for property developers, introduced in August [number withheld] by the People's Bank of China (PBoC) and the Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development (MOHURD). Details, thresholds and verification status: [[Three Red Lines Policy]].

## Motivation

- **Policy relevance:** the policy imposed hard, ratio-based thresholds on reported balance-sheet metrics. Hard thresholds on reported numbers create an incentive to manage the reported numbers rather than the underlying economic leverage (e.g. off-balance-sheet joint ventures, debt disguised as equity 明股实债). Press reports describe exactly such practices ([[Press Evidence Off-Balance-Sheet Debt]]).
- **Gap:** existing studies examine direct effects on leverage, bond spreads, performance and spillovers; whether the thresholds were *circumvented* has, per the project's search so far, not been studied systematically ([[Literature Gap]]).
- **PI's choice of angle:** Anton deliberately excluded pensions as a topic. He is aiming for investment banking, so a **corporate-finance / balance-sheet focus** is intended.

## Approach in one paragraph

Two complementary pieces of evidence (details in [[Empirical Design]]):
[number withheld]. **Bunching** of the three red-line ratios just below [number withheld] % / [number withheld] % / [number withheld] after the policy vs. before (Kleven [number withheld], [[Kleven [number withheld] Bunching]]).
[number withheld]. **Dose-response DiD:** treatment intensity = number of lines breached before the policy; outcomes are balance-sheet items that indicate off-balance-sheet shifting (minority equity, minority equity–profit gap, JV/associate investments, perpetual bonds) alongside reported leverage. An **adjusted leverage** measure compares economic with reported leverage.
