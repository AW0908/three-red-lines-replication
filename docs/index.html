Meeting the Red Lines

Threshold targeting and minority equity among Chinese property developers under the Three Red Lines policy.

Anton Wilhelmi · Modern Tools for Policy Analysis

Preliminary findings Explore tables and figures Code and data

Results on this page are preliminary and stem from a research proposal. They describe patterns; causal attribution to the policy is still being tested.

The policy

Three ratios decide how fast a developer may borrow

China's property sector runs on credit: about a quarter of all bank loans go to real estate (Ding et al. 2017, IMF Working Paper 17/248). In August 2020, the People's Bank of China and the Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development introduced three balance-sheet limits for developers, with the aim of controlled deleveraging and a stable housing market.

Liabilities and assets in RL1 are both net of advance receipts from home buyers.

The question

Did developers get below 70% by deleveraging, or only on paper?

I investigate whether developers targeted the RL1 threshold and which balance-sheet adjustments and capital terms underlie their reported compliance. The answer matters because ratio-based rules work only if reported ratios track risk.

Real

Economic leverage falls

  • Repay debt: N falls
  • Raise risk-bearing equity: E rises, often as minority interests in project companies
Cosmetic

Economic leverage unchanged

  • Partner equity with buyback or guaranteed return
  • Equity from the developer's own group
  • Perpetual bonds booked as equity
RL1 = NN + E ≤ 70%

N: liabilities net of advance receipts. E: total equity, including minority interests.
New partner equity raises E on both sides. Public balance sheets cannot tell real from cosmetic equity.

Three research designs

Targeting

Difference-in-bunching

Did excess mass just below 70% grow more for developers than for firms the rule does not cover? Excess mass is measured as in Kleven (2016).

Mechanism

Frozen minority interests

Recompute RL1 with minority interests held at their end-2019 level. Would developers still comply?

Substance

Contract audit

Code company filings around each crossing for buybacks, guaranteed returns, put rights, guarantees and shareholder loans.

Preliminary findings

Developers comply on paper; what stands behind it is hard to see

FINDING 1 · TARGETING

Developers bunch just below 70% after the policy

Before 2020, developers' RL1 values spread across the line. Afterwards a spike appears just below it. The rise is larger than among unregulated firms in the same market.

4.34rise in excess mass relative to unregulated firms, 95% confidence interval 1.97 to 7.54

A-share and Hong Kong developers, each compared with unregulated firms in their own market. Among A-shares alone the interval includes zero, and the 2021 sector distress could also move developers.

FINDING 2 · MECHANISM

Without minority-equity growth since 2019, the bunching disappears

Holding minority interests at their end-2019 level, the spike below 70% vanishes and the compliant share of firm-half-years falls from 51.2% to 39.7% (602 firm-half-years, 129 developers).

11.5percentage points lower compliant share, 95% confidence interval 6.9 to 16.2

This shows that compliance depends on minority equity added since 2019, not that the policy caused that equity. Before the policy, the same calculation already moves compliance by 4.1 percentage points.

FINDING 3 · SUBSTANCE

Public filings rarely reveal whether partner equity is real

Around the 33 crossings, company filings describe 249 transactions that add equity. Only 10 disclose their terms, and just one of them carries a fixed or guaranteed return.

10 of 249transactions with known terms

Exits at market or project value keep project risk with the partner, so they are not debt in substance. If 28% of the new minority equity were debt-like, the bunching would disappear. Unknown terms are not proof of debt; they show what disclosure leaves open.

Research process

Every step is on the record

The analysis was carried out as a series of task cards. AI agents executed each card. AI reviewers checked data, estimation, consistency and language. Reviews are process-independent: each role starts a fresh read-only session. They are not model-independent: some reviewers use the same model family as the worker. I approved, returned or overrode each result with a stated reason. Cards, review reports, decisions and the AI-use log are published as they were written.
